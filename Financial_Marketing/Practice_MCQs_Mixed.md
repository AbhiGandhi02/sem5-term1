**Q1.** Fixed costs rise by 10%, while selling price and variable cost per unit stay the same. What happens to the break-even point (units) and the P/V ratio?
- A) BEP is unchanged; the P/V ratio falls
- B) BEP rises by 10%; the P/V ratio is unchanged
- C) BEP falls by 10%; the margin of safety rises
- D) BEP and the P/V ratio both rise by 10%

> ✅ **Answer: B.** P/V ratio = Contribution ÷ Sales depends only on SP and VC, so it does not move. BEP = FC ÷ CM per unit, so a 10% rise in FC raises BEP by exactly 10%. 📖 Session 6

**Q2.** A machine costs ₹10,00,000, has a residual value of ₹1,00,000 and a useful life of 6 years (SLM). It is sold at the end of Year 4 for ₹4,50,000. What is the result on sale?
- A) Loss of ₹5,50,000
- B) Loss of ₹50,000
- C) Gain of ₹50,000
- D) Gain of ₹1,16,667

> ✅ **Answer: C.** Annual depreciation = (10,00,000 − 1,00,000) ÷ 6 = ₹1,50,000. Carrying amount after 4 years = 10,00,000 − 4 × 1,50,000 = ₹4,00,000. Gain = Sale price − Carrying amount = 4,50,000 − 4,00,000 = **₹50,000**. Option D ignores the residual value; Option A compares the sale price with the original cost. 📖 Sessions 1 + 2

**Q3.** FCFF is ₹105 Cr. Interest expense is ₹20 Cr, the tax rate is 25%, and during the year the company raised ₹30 Cr of new debt and repaid ₹10 Cr. What is FCFE?
- A) ₹70 Cr
- B) ₹110 Cr
- C) ₹120 Cr
- D) ₹105 Cr

> ✅ **Answer: B.** Net borrowing = 30 − 10 = ₹20 Cr. FCFE = FCFF − Interest × (1 − T) + Net borrowing = 105 − 15 + 20 = **₹110 Cr**. Option D deducts the full pre-tax interest; Option C adds gross new debt; Option A subtracts net borrowing. 📖 Session 11

**Q4.** A ₹10L market-linked note has 70% participation in index gains, capped at 30% total return, over 4 years. The index rises 55%. What are the maturity value and the simple annual return?

- B) ₹15.50L; 13.75% a year
- C) ₹13.00L; 6.78% a year
- D) ₹13.85L; 9.62% a year

> ✅ **Answer: A.** Participation return = 70% × 55% = 38.5%, which is above the 30% cap, so the return is 30%. Value = 10 × 1.30 = **₹13.00L**. Simple annual return = 30% ÷ 4 = **7.50%**. Option D ignores the cap; Option B assumes full participation with no cap; Option C gives the compound rate, not the simple rate. 📖 Session 16

**Q5.** Bank X offers 12.5% compounded annually. Bank Y offers 12% compounded monthly. What is Bank Y's effective annual rate, and which bank pays a depositor more?
- A) 1.00%; Bank X pays more
- B) 12.00%; Bank X pays more
- C) 12.68%; Bank Y pays more
- D) 12.36%; Bank X pays more

> ✅ **Answer: C.** EAR = (1 + 0.12 ÷ 12)¹² − 1 = 1.01¹² − 1 = **12.68%**, which beats 12.5%. Option B treats the APR as the effective rate; Option D uses semi-annual compounding; Option A is the monthly periodic rate. 📖 Session 7

**Q6.** Weights: equity 60%, debt 40%. Cost of equity 14%, pre-tax cost of debt 9%, tax rate 25%. What is the WACC?
- A) 11.10%
- B) 10.38%
- C) 9.65%
- D) 12.00%

> ✅ **Answer: A.** WACC = We × Ke + Wd × Kd × (1 − T) = 0.6 × 14% + 0.4 × 9% × 0.75 = 8.40% + 2.70% = **11.10%**. Option D forgets the tax shield; Option C swaps the weights; Option B takes a simple average. 📖 Session 12

**Q7.** A company borrows money and uses it to buy back shares. Assume its net profit margin and asset turnover stay the same. Using DuPont, what happens to ROE?
- A) ROE rises, because the equity multiplier increases
- B) ROE is unchanged, because NPM and asset turnover are unchanged
- C) ROE falls, because total assets increase
- D) ROE falls, because the equity multiplier decreases

> ✅ **Answer: A.** A buyback shrinks equity while debt rises, so Assets ÷ Equity (the equity multiplier) goes up. With the other two DuPont factors fixed, ROE rises. Option B forgets the third factor. This is why leverage boosts ROE, and also risk. 📖 Session 4

**Q8.** In an FCFF model, the PV of Year 1–5 cash flows is ₹2,100 Cr and the PV of the terminal value is ₹5,400 Cr. Net debt is ₹1,500 Cr and there are 30 Cr shares. What is the implied value per share?

**Q13.** A firm has DIO of 50 days and DSO of 40 days. It starts paying suppliers faster, so DPO falls from 45 to 30 days. What happens to the cash conversion cycle?
- A) It shortens by 15 days (45 → 30)
- B) It lengthens by 15 days (45 → 60)

> ✅ **Answer: B.** Cycle = DIO + DSO − DPO = 50 + 40 − 45 = 45 days before, and 50 + 40 − 30 = 60 days after. A lower DPO means cash leaves sooner, so the cycle lengthens. Option C is wrong because the operating cycle (DIO + DSO) does not include DPO. 📖 Session 3

**Q15.** An analyst discounts a company's FCFE at its WACC instead of its cost of equity. What is the likely effect on the equity value?
- A) No effect, because both rates give the same answer
- B) It is overstated only if the company has no debt
- C) It is overstated, because WACC is usually below the cost of equity
- D) It is understated, because WACC is usually above the cost of equity

> ✅ **Answer: C.** Match the cash flow to its owners: FCFE belongs to shareholders, so it is discounted at Ke. WACC blends in cheaper after-tax debt, so it is lower, and a lower rate inflates value. Option B is backwards: with no debt, WACC equals Ke and there is no error. 📖 Sessions 11 + 12

**Q22.** A bond's credit spread stays at 3%, but the expected recovery rate falls from 40% to 20%. What happens to the market-implied probability of default?
