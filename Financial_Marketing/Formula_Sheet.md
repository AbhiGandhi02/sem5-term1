## Session 1: Introduction to Financial Accounting
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Accounting Equation | `Assets = Liabilities + Equity` | Must balance after every transaction (dual aspect) |
| 2 | Equity (residual claim) | `Equity = Assets − Liabilities` | What owners keep after paying all outsiders |
| 3 | Equity of a proprietorship | `Equity = Owner's Capital + Profit − Drawings` | Drawings reduce equity; they are not an expense |
| 4 | Equity of a company | `Equity = Share Capital + Retained Earnings + Other Reserves` | Company form of owners' claim |
| 5 | Change in equity from operations (corrected slide formula) | `ΔEquity = Income − Expenses + Gains − Losses` | Gives profit for the period, not total equity |
| 6 | Gross Profit (matching concept) | `Gross Profit = Sales − COGS` | COGS (Cost of Goods Sold) counts only units sold, not all purchases |
| 7 | Cost of Goods Sold | `COGS = Units sold × Cost per unit` | Unsold units stay as inventory (an asset) |
| 8 | Closing inventory | `Closing Inventory = Units unsold × Cost per unit` | Value of stock carried to next period |
| 9 | Carrying amount of an asset | `Carrying Amount = Cost − Depreciation` | Book value used to find gain or loss on sale |
| 10 | Gain or loss on sale of an asset | `Gain (or Loss) = Sale Price − Carrying Amount` | Positive = gain, negative = loss; both hit equity |


## Session 2: Financial Statements
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Depreciable Amount | `Depreciable Amount = Cost − Residual Value` | Total amount of PPE (Property, Plant and Equipment) cost to be used up |
| 2 | Straight Line depreciation | `Annual Depreciation = Depreciable Amount ÷ Useful Life` | SLM (Straight Line Method): same charge every year |
| 3 | Reducing Balance depreciation | `Annual Depreciation = Opening Balance × Depreciation Rate` | RBM (Reducing Balance Method) / WDV (Written Down Value): higher charge early |
| 4 | Closing book value (each year) | `Closing Balance = Opening Balance − Depreciation for the year` | Next year's opening balance |
| 5 | Intangible: cost model | `Carrying Amount = Cost − Accumulated Amortisation − Accumulated Impairment` | Book value of patents, software, etc. |
| 6 | Intangible: revaluation model | `Carrying Amount = Fair Value at revaluation − Later Amortisation − Later Impairment` | Uses fair value instead of cost |
| 7 | Annual amortisation (straight line) | `Amortisation = Cost of Intangible ÷ Useful Life` | Depreciation of an intangible asset |
| 8 | Share capital | `Share Capital = Number of Shares × Face Value` | Recorded at face/par value only |
| 9 | Securities premium | `Securities Premium = Number of Shares × (Issue Price − Face Value)` | Extra received above face value |
| 10 | Operating Profit | `EBIT = Gross Profit − Operating Expenses` | EBIT (Earnings Before Interest and Taxes): core business profit |
| 11 | Profit Before Tax | `PBT = Operating Profit + Other Income − Other Expenses` | PBT (Profit Before Tax) |
| 12 | Profit After Tax | `PAT = PBT − Income Tax` | PAT (Profit After Tax): the bottom line (net profit) |
| 13 | Cash reconciliation | `Closing Cash = Opening Cash + CFO + CFI + CFF` | CFO (Cash Flow from Operating Activities), CFI (Cash Flow from Investing Activities), CFF (Cash Flow from Financing Activities) |
| 14 | CFO: indirect method | `CFO = Net Profit + Non-cash Expenses − Gain on Asset Sale + Loss on Asset Sale − ΔReceivables − ΔInventory + ΔPayables` | Converts accrual profit into operating cash |
| 15 | Working capital rule (CFO) | `CA ↑ = outflow (−); CA ↓ = inflow (+); CL ↑ = inflow (+); CL ↓ = outflow (−)` | Quick sign rule for indirect-method adjustments; where CA = Current Assets, CL = Current Liabilities |


## Session 3: Financial Ratio Analysis (Part 1)
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Current Ratio | `Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities` | Short-term liquidity; about 2 is comfortable, below 1 is risky |
| 2 | Quick Assets | `Quick Assets = Current Assets − Inventory` | Assets convertible to cash without selling stock |
| 3 | Quick (Acid-Test) Ratio | `Quick Ratio = Quick Assets ÷ Current Liabilities` | About 1 is good; pays bills without selling inventory |
| 4 | Cash Ratio | `Cash Ratio = (Cash + Cash Equivalents + Marketable Securities) ÷ Current Liabilities` | Strictest liquidity test; higher is safer, too high means idle cash |
| 5 | Average (for any balance) | `Average = (Opening + Closing) ÷ 2` | Use average stock when dividing a flow by a balance |
| 6 | Inventory Turnover | `Inventory Turnover = COGS ÷ Average Inventory` | Higher = goods sell faster |
| 7 | Inventory Holding Period | `DIO = (Average Inventory ÷ COGS) × 365` | DIO (Days Inventory Outstanding): lower is better. Shortcut: Days = 365 ÷ Turnover (same for DSO (Days Sales Outstanding) and DPO (Days Payables Outstanding)) |
| 8 | Receivables Turnover | `Receivables Turnover = Net Credit Sales ÷ Average Trade Receivables` | Higher = customers pay faster |
| 9 | Average Collection Period | `DSO = (Average Trade Receivables ÷ Net Credit Sales) × 365` | DSO: lower is better; compare with credit terms |
| 10 | Payables Turnover | `Payables Turnover = Credit Purchases (or COGS) ÷ Average Trade Payables` | How often suppliers are paid in a year |
| 11 | Average Payment Period | `DPO = (Average Trade Payables ÷ Credit Purchases) × 365` | DPO: higher = free supplier credit, within limits |
| 12 | Operating Cycle (textbook) | `Operating Cycle = DIO + DSO` | Days from buying stock to collecting cash |
| 13 | Working Capital / Cash Conversion Cycle | `Cycle = DIO + DSO − DPO` | Lower or negative is better; less working capital needed |
| 14 | Debt-to-Equity Ratio | `D/E = Total Debt ÷ Shareholders' Equity` | D/E (Debt-to-Equity): lower = less financial risk; use the debt definition asked |
| 15 | Interest Coverage Ratio | `ICR = EBIT ÷ Interest Expense` | ICR (Interest Coverage Ratio): higher is safer; below 1 is danger |
| 16 | EBIT from PBT | `EBIT = PBT + Interest` | Common exam trap when only PBT is given |


## Session 4: Financial Ratio Analysis (Part 2)
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gross Profit Margin | `GPM = (Revenue − Cost of Sales) ÷ Revenue × 100` | GPM (Gross Profit Margin): higher = better product profitability |
| 2 | Operating Profit Margin | `OPM = EBIT ÷ Revenue × 100` | OPM (Operating Profit Margin): core operating efficiency; higher is better |
| 3 | Net Profit Margin | `NPM = PAT ÷ Revenue × 100` | NPM (Net Profit Margin): profit kept from each rupee of sales |
| 4 | Return on Capital Employed | `ROCE = EBIT ÷ Capital Employed × 100` | ROCE (Return on Capital Employed): return on long-term capital; higher is better |
| 5 | Capital Employed | `Capital Employed = Total Assets − Current Liabilities, or Equity + Long-term Debt` | Both versions give the same number |
| 6 | Return on Assets | `ROA = PAT ÷ Average Total Assets × 100` | ROA (Return on Assets): how productively assets earn profit |
| 7 | Return on Equity | `ROE = PAT ÷ Average Shareholders' Equity × 100` | ROE (Return on Equity): return to owners; higher is better |
| 8 | DuPont analysis | `ROE = NPM × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier` | Asset Turnover = Revenue ÷ Assets; Equity Multiplier = Assets ÷ Equity |
| 9 | Return on Investment | `ROI = (Gain from Investment − Investment Cost) ÷ Investment Cost × 100` | ROI (Return on Investment): return on one specific investment |
| 10 | Earnings Per Share | `EPS = (PAT − Preference Dividend) ÷ Number of Ordinary Shares` | Profit belonging to each ordinary share; where EPS = Earnings Per Share |
| 11 | Price-Earnings Ratio | `P/E = Market Price Per Share ÷ EPS` | P/E (Price-to-Earnings): high = growth expectations or overvaluation |
| 12 | Dividend Per Share | `DPS = Total Ordinary Dividends ÷ Number of Ordinary Shares` | DPS (Dividend Per Share): cash paid per share |
| 13 | Dividend Payout Ratio | `Payout = DPS ÷ EPS × 100` | Share of earnings paid out as dividends |
| 14 | Retention Ratio | `Retention = 100% − Payout` | Share of earnings reinvested in the business |
| 15 | Dividend Yield | `Dividend Yield = DPS ÷ Market Price Per Share × 100` | Cash return from dividends at today's price |
| 16 | Market Capitalisation | `Market Cap = Market Price Per Share × Shares Outstanding` | Market value of the company's equity |
| 17 | Enterprise Value (corrected slide formula) | `EV = Market Cap + Total Debt − Cash & Cash Equivalents` | EV (Enterprise Value): price to buy the whole business |


## Session 5: Financial Planning & Analysis 1 (Management Accounting & Cost Concepts)
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost per unit | `Total production cost ÷ Units produced` | Average cost of making one unit; basis for pricing |
| 2 | Total cost (by function) | `Production + Administration + Selling + Distribution` | Full cost of running the business, grouped by where incurred |
| 3 | Fixed cost per unit | `Total Fixed Cost ÷ Units` | Falls as output rises (economies of scale); total stays constant |
| 4 | Total variable cost | `Variable cost per unit × Output` | Rises in proportion to output; per-unit stays constant |
| 5 | Semi-variable cost | `Fixed part + (Variable rate × Activity)` | Starts at the fixed amount even at zero activity |
| 6 | Variable rate (high-low method) | `(Cost at high activity − Cost at low activity) ÷ (High activity − Low activity)` | Splits a semi-variable cost into its variable rate per unit |
| 7 | Opportunity cost | `Benefit sacrificed from the next best alternative` | Relevant to decisions; include it, unlike sunk cost |
| 8 | Total production cost | `DM + DL + Production overheads` | Full manufacturing cost of a product, DL (Direct Labour); where DM = Direct Material |
| 9 | Prime cost | `Direct Material + Direct Labour (+ Direct expenses)` | All directly traceable costs of a product |
| 10 | OAR (Overhead Absorption Rate) | `Budgeted Overhead ÷ Budgeted Activity` | Overhead charged per unit, machine-hour or labour-hour |
| 11 | Full production cost per unit | `Direct cost per unit + OAR` | Unit cost including its share of factory overheads |
| 12 | MC (Marginal Cost) | `Change in Total Cost ÷ Change in Output` | Extra cost of one more unit; roughly the variable cost per unit |
| 13 | Contribution | `Sales − Variable Cost` | Money left to cover fixed costs, then profit |
| 14 | Contribution per unit | `SP − Variable cost per unit (≈ SP − MC)` | Each unit's addition to fixed-cost cover and profit; where SP = Selling Price |
| 15 | Profit (marginal costing) | `Total Contribution − Fixed Costs` | Profit appears only after fixed costs are covered |
| 16 | Minimum price (spare capacity) | `Minimum price = MC` | With idle capacity, accept any order priced above MC |
| 17 | Minimum price (capacity constraint) | `MC + Opportunity Cost` | When full, price must also cover contribution given up |
| 18 | Opportunity cost per unit (capacity constraint) | `Contribution lost from the displaced sale = Alternative price − MC` | Contribution you give up by using scarce capacity for this order |


## Session 6: CVP (Cost-Volume-Profit) Analysis
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | COGS (absorption costing) | `Cost of production + Opening stock − Closing stock` | Cost of the units actually sold this period |
| 2 | Under-absorbed overhead | `Actual fixed production OH − Absorbed OH (if actual is higher)` | Deducted from gross profit; where OH = Overhead |
| 3 | Over-absorbed overhead | `Absorbed OH − Actual fixed production OH (if absorbed is higher)` | Added back to gross profit |
| 4 | Net profit (absorption costing) | `Gross profit ± Over/under-absorbed OH − Selling & admin overhead` | Final profit under full costing |
| 5 | Absorption vs marginal profit gap | `Closing stock units × Fixed OH per unit (no opening stock)` | Absorption profit is higher when stock rises; equal when production = sales |
| 6 | Sales revenue | `SP per unit × Units sold` | Total money from sales |
| 7 | P/V (Profit-Volume) ratio | `(Contribution ÷ Sales) × 100` | Contribution per ₹100 of sales; higher is better |
| 8 | P/V ratio (two periods) | `Change in profit ÷ Change in sales × 100` | Finds P/V when FC (Fixed Cost) is unchanged between periods |
| 9 | BEP (Break-Even Point) (units) | `FC ÷ CM per unit` | Units needed for zero profit; lower BEP means less risk; where CM = Contribution Margin |
| 10 | BEP (sales value) | `FC ÷ P/V ratio` | Sales value needed for zero profit |
| 11 | Break-even condition | `Total Revenue = Total Cost, so Profit = 0` | No profit, no loss |
| 12 | MoS (Margin of Safety) (units) | `Actual units sold − Break-even units` | How many units sales can fall before a loss |
| 13 | MoS (value) | `Actual sales − Break-even sales` | Sales cushion in ₹ above BEP |
| 14 | MoS % | `(MoS ÷ Actual sales) × 100` | Higher MoS % = lower risk |
| 15 | Units for target profit | `(FC + Target profit) ÷ CM per unit` | Volume needed to earn a chosen profit |
| 16 | Profit (MoS shortcut) | `MoS (value) × P/V ratio` or `MoS units × CM` | Sales above BEP turn entirely into profit |
| 17 | Profit (P/V shortcut) | `Sales × P/V ratio − FC` | Profit directly from sales and P/V |
| 18 | Total cost line (break-even chart) | `Total cost = FC + VC × Units` | Starts at FC, not zero; crosses revenue at BEP; where VC = Variable Cost |


## Session 7: TVM (Time Value of Money)
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FV (Future Value) (compounding) | `FV = PV × (1 + r)^n`; compounding factor = `(1 + r)^n` | What money today grows to, r = rate, n = periods; where PV = Present Value |
| 2 | Simple interest (total) | `Principal × r × n` | Interest on principal only; no interest on interest |
| 3 | Interest on interest | `Compound interest = Simple interest + Interest on interest` | Extra earned purely from compounding; grows every year |
| 4 | PV (discounting) | `PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)^n`; discounting factor = `1 ÷ (1 + r)^n` | Today's value of future money; falls as r or n rises |
| 5 | APR (Annual Percentage Rate) (nominal) | `Periodic rate × Number of periods per year` | Headline rate; ignores compounding within the year |
| 6 | EAR (Effective Annual Rate) | `(1 + Periodic rate)^(periods per year) − 1` | True annual cost or return; always ≥ APR |
| 7 | PV of uneven cash flows | `PV = Σ CFt ÷ (1 + r)^t` | Discount each flow by its own year, then add |
| 8 | PV of ordinary annuity (PVIFA (Present Value Interest Factor of an Annuity)) | `PV = A × PVIFA, PVIFA = [1 − (1 + r)^(−n)] ÷ r` | Lump sum today equal to n end-of-period payments A |
| 9 | FV of ordinary annuity (FVIFA (Future Value Interest Factor of an Annuity)) | `FV = A × FVIFA, FVIFA = [(1 + r)^n − 1] ÷ r` | Fund value after n end-of-period deposits |
| 10 | Annuity due | `Ordinary annuity value × (1 + r)` | Payments at start of period are worth more |
| 11 | Loan instalment (EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment)) | `Instalment = Loan amount ÷ PVIFA` | Equal payment that repays a loan over n periods |
| 12 | Constant perpetuity | `PV = CF ÷ r` | Value of a fixed cash flow forever; where CF = Cash Flow |
| 13 | Growing perpetuity (Gordon model) | `PV = CF1 ÷ (r − g), valid only if r > g` | Value of a flow growing at g forever; where g = growth rate |
| 14 | Next year's cash flow | `CF1 = CF0 × (1 + g)` | Grow this year's flow before using the growing-perpetuity formula |
| 15 | TV (Terminal Value) | `TVn = CFn × (1 + g) ÷ (r − g)` | Value at year n of all cash flows after the forecast |
| 16 | PV of terminal value | `PV of TV = TVn ÷ (1 + r)^n` | TV sits at year n, so always discount it back. Discount TV with the Year-n factor, not Year n−1 |


## Session 8: Capital Budgeting Techniques
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | NPV (Net Present Value) | `NPV = −I0 + CF1 ÷ (1 + r)^1 + … + CFn ÷ (1 + r)^n = Σ CFt ÷ (1 + r)^t − I0` | ₹ value created today; accept if > 0, reject if < 0; where I0 = initial investment |
| 2 | NPV with equal annual cash flows | `NPV = Annual CF × PVIFA(r, n) − I0` | Shortcut when inflows are an annuity |
| 3 | IRR (Internal Rate of Return) (definition) | `0 = Σ CFt ÷ (1 + IRR)^t − Initial Investment` | Rate where NPV = 0; accept if IRR > cost of capital |
| 4 | IRR by interpolation | `IRR = L + [NPV_L ÷ (NPV_L − NPV_H)] × (H − L)` | Estimates IRR between a lower rate L and a higher rate H |
| 5 | Payback period | `Years before recovery + Remaining amount ÷ Next year's cash flow` | Time to recover investment; shorter = less liquidity risk; ignores TVM |
| 6 | Discounted payback period | `Years before recovery + Remaining amount ÷ Next year's discounted CF` | Recovery time using discounted flows; always longer than simple payback |
| 7 | PI (Profitability Index) | `PV of future cash flows ÷ Initial investment` | Value per ₹1 invested; accept if > 1 (same as NPV > 0) |


## Session 9: Cost of Capital (Cost of Equity, Risk, Beta & Alpha)
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost of Equity by CAPM (Capital Asset Pricing Model) | `Ke = Rf + β × (Rm − Rf)` | Minimum return shareholders require for the stock's market risk, Rf (Risk-free Rate), Rm (Expected Market Return); where Ke = Cost of Equity |
| 2 | Market Risk Premium | `MRP = Rm − Rf` | Extra return demanded for bearing market risk. Also called ERP (Equity Risk Premium); where MRP = Market Risk Premium |
| 3 | Beta (Covariance / Variance method) | `β = Cov(Ri, Rm) ÷ Var(Rm)` | Stock's sensitivity to market moves, Var (Variance), Ri (Return on stock i); where Cov = Covariance |
| 4 | Covariance (sample) | `Cov = Σ[(Ri − R̄i) × (Rm − R̄m)] ÷ (n − 1)` | How stock and market returns move together |
| 5 | Variance of market (sample) | `Var = Σ(Rm − R̄m)² ÷ (n − 1)` | Market's own volatility; the (n − 1) cancels when computing β |
| 6 | Regression (market model) | `Ri = α + β × Rm + ε` | α = market-unrelated return, β = slope (systematic risk), ε = firm-specific risk |
| 7 | Beta (regression slope shortcut) | `β = [nΣ(Ri·Rm) − ΣRi × ΣRm] ÷ [nΣRm² − (ΣRm)²]` | Beta directly from sums of raw return data |
| 8 | Alpha (Jensen's alpha) | `α = R_actual − [Rf + β × (Rm − Rf)]` | Excess return over CAPM expectation; positive = manager skill |
| 9 | Cost of Equity by DGM (Dividend Growth Model) | `Ke = D1 ÷ P0 + g` | Dividend yield plus growth; only for dividend payers, sensitive to g |


## Session 10: Cost of Debt – Bonds & Stock Valuation
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coupon amount | `C = Coupon Rate × Face Value (÷ 2 if semi-annual)` | Rupee interest paid each period |
| 2 | Coupon (plain vanilla) bond price | `P = C/(1+r)¹ + C/(1+r)² + … + (C + F)/(1+r)^T` | Price = PV of all coupons + PV of face value |
| 3 | Zero-coupon bond price | `P = F ÷ (1+r)^T` | Deep-discount price; whole return comes at redemption |
| 4 | Zero-coupon investor gain | `Gain = F − P` | Total return on a zero-coupon bond held to maturity |
| 5 | YTM (Yield to Maturity) (approximation) | `YTM ≈ [C + (F − P) ÷ n] ÷ [(F + P) ÷ 2]` | Approximate total return if held to maturity; price below F means YTM above coupon |
| 6 | Current yield | `Current Yield = C ÷ P` | Coupon income only; ignores capital gain or loss |
| 7 | Pre-tax cost of debt | `Pre-tax Kd = YTM` | Market's required return on the debt; tax-deductible interest makes effective cost lower; where Kd = Cost of Debt |
| 8 | Two-stage growth model | `P0 = Σ (t=1 to n) Dt ÷ (1+r)^t + Pn ÷ (1+r)^n` | PV of high-growth dividends + PV of terminal price (exponent n, not t). Pn = D(n+1) ÷ (r − g2), the Gordon price at year n |
| 9 | Stage-1 PV (closed form, growing annuity) | `PV = D1 × [1 − ((1 + g1) ÷ (1 + r))^n] ÷ (r − g1)` | Same result as discounting each high-growth dividend separately |


## Session 11: Valuation & Forecasting – 1 (Free Cash Flow, FCFF (Free Cash Flow to Firm) & FCFE (Free Cash Flow to Equity))
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Value of an asset | `Value = PV of expected future economic benefits` | Core idea behind all intrinsic valuation |
| 2 | FCF (Free Cash Flow) | `FCF = OCF − CapEx` | Cash truly free after needed investment, CapEx (Capital Expenditure); where OCF = Operating Cash Flow |
| 3 | NOPAT (Net Operating Profit After Tax) | `NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − T)` | Operating profit as if debt-free, T = tax rate |
| 4 | FCFF | `FCFF = EBIT(1 − T) + Depreciation − CapEx − ΔNWC` | Cash available to all capital providers; where NWC = Net Working Capital |
| 5 | FCFF (CFO shortcut) | `FCFF ≈ CFO − CapEx` | Quick estimate from reported cash flow |
| 6 | FCFE | `FCFE = NI + Depreciation − CapEx − ΔNWC + Net Borrowing` | Cash left for shareholders only; where NI = Net Income |
| 7 | Net Borrowing | `Net Borrowing = New Debt Raised − Debt Repaid` | Added to FCFE; negative when debt is repaid |
| 8 | FCFE from FCFF | `FCFE = FCFF − Interest × (1 − T) + Net Borrowing` | Links firm cash flow to equity cash flow |
| 9 | FCFE (CFO route, slide table) | `FCFE ≈ CFO − CapEx − Interest + Net Borrowing` | Practical estimate from reported cash flows |
| 10 | EV to equity | `Equity Value = EV − Debt + Cash` | FCFF at WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital) gives EV; FCFE at Ke gives equity directly |
| 11 | P/E multiple | `P/E = Price ÷ EPS` | Equity multiple for relative valuation |
| 12 | EV/EBITDA multiple | `EV/EBITDA = Enterprise Value ÷ EBITDA` | Firm multiple, ignores capital structure; where EBITDA = Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation and Amortisation |


## Session 12: DCF (Discounted Cash Flow) Valuation Model – FCFF (Reliance) & FCFE (HDFC Bank)
| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Base-year FCFE (bank version) | `FCFE = NI + D&A − Capex − Net loan-book investment + Net borrowing` | For banks, loan-book growth replaces working capital; where D&A = Depreciation & Amortisation |
| 2 | After-tax cost of debt | `After-tax Rd = Rd × (1 − T)` | Tax shield lowers the effective cost of debt; where Rd = pre-tax cost of debt |
| 3 | WACC | `WACC = We × Re + Wd × Rd × (1 − T)` | Discount rate for FCFF; weights We + Wd must sum to 100% |
| 4 | Enterprise Value (FCFF route) | `EV = Σ PV(FCFF, Years 1–5) + PV(TV)` | Value of whole firm for lenders and shareholders |
| 5 | Net Debt | `Net Debt = Debt − Cash` | Lenders' net claim removed to reach equity |
| 6 | Equity Value (FCFE route) | `Equity Value = Σ PV(FCFE, Years 1–5) + PV(TV)` | Equity directly; banks have no EV or WACC step |
| 7 | Implied value per share | `Value per share = Equity Value ÷ Shares outstanding` | ₹ Crore ÷ Crore shares = ₹ per share |


## Sessions 13–14: Financial Derivatives

| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contract exposure (one lot) | `Exposure = Index level × Lot size` | Notional value you control, e.g. 24,000 × 75 = ₹18 lakh |
| 2 | Margin required | `Margin = Exposure × Margin %` | Deposit posted to hold the position |
| 3 | Leverage effect on margin | `Loss as % of margin = Index move % ÷ Margin %` | 1% move at 10% margin = 10% of your margin |
| 4 | Forward points | `Forward points = Forward rate − Spot rate` | Reflects interest-rate gap between currencies, not a forecast |
| 5 | Forward / futures fair price (cost-of-carry) | `F = S × (1 + r × T) − Dividends` | No-arbitrage price: spot plus carrying cost minus income |
| 6 | Cash-and-carry arbitrage profit | `Profit = F(market) − F(fair)` | Future overpriced: borrow, buy spot, sell future |
| 7 | Reverse cash-and-carry profit | `Profit = F(fair) − F(market)` | Future underpriced: short spot, invest, buy future |
| 8 | Long futures profit/loss | `P/L = (Settlement or Close − Entry price) × Lot size` | Linear payoff; short is opposite; where P/L = Profit/Loss |
| 9 | Daily MTM (Mark-to-Market) | `Day P/L = (Today's settlement − Yesterday's settlement) × Lot size` | Cash moves daily; cumulative sum equals single-settlement P/L |
| 10 | Basis | `Basis = Futures price − Spot price` | Shrinks to zero at expiry |
| 11 | Rollover cost | `Roll cost = (Next-month price − Near-month price) × Lot size` | Cost of extending a long futures position |
| 12 | Futures hedge ratio (lots) | `Lots = Portfolio value ÷ (Index level × Lot size)` | Number of index futures to sell for a full hedge |
| 13 | Interest rate swap: net settlement | `Swap net = (Floating rate received − Fixed rate paid) × Notional` | Only net interest is exchanged, never the notional |
| 14 | Swapped loan cost | `Effective cost = Fixed swap rate + Loan spread over MIBOR` | Floating MIBOR (Mumbai Interbank Offered Rate) cancels; cost is fixed |
| 15 | Call payoff at expiry | `Call value = max(S − K, 0)` | Right to buy at strike K |
| 16 | Put payoff at expiry | `Put value = max(K − S, 0)` | Right to sell at strike K |
| 17 | Long option profit | `Profit = Payoff − Premium` | Buyer's maximum loss is the premium |
| 18 | Short (written) option profit | `Writer P/L = Premium − Payoff` | Gain capped at premium; short call loss unlimited |
| 19 | Moneyness (call) | `ITM: S > K · ATM: S ≈ K · OTM: S < K` | ITM (In-the-Money), ATM (At-the-Money), OTM (Out-of-the-Money) |
| 20 | Moneyness (put) | `ITM: S < K · ATM: S ≈ K · OTM: S > K` | Reverse of the call rule |
| 21 | Option premium split | `Premium = Intrinsic value + Time value` | ATM options have the most time value |
| 22 | Long call break-even | `BE = K + Premium` | BE (Break-Even) price at expiry |
| 23 | Long put break-even | `BE = K − Premium` | Price must fall below this to profit. Same value is the long put max profit and short put max loss |
| 24 | Option leverage | `Return on call = (Payoff − Premium) ÷ Premium` | Small premium magnifies % gains and losses |
| 25 | Price process (lognormal) | `dS/S = μ dt + σ dW` | Prices stay positive; spread grows with √T |
| 26 | Black-Scholes call price | `C = S e^(−qT) N(d1) − K e^(−rT) N(d2)` | European call value; expected return does not appear |
| 27 | Black-Scholes put price | `P = K e^(−rT) N(−d2) − S e^(−qT) N(−d1)` | European put value |
| 28 | d1 | `d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r − q + σ²/2) × T] ÷ (σ√T)` | Distance from strike in SD (Standard Deviation) units, plus growth adjustment |
| 29 | d2 | `d2 = d1 − σ√T` | N(d2) = risk-neutral chance of finishing ITM |
| 30 | Put-call parity | `C − P = S e^(−qT) − K e^(−rT)` | Links call and put prices on same strike/expiry |
| 31 | ATM call rule of thumb | `ATM call ≈ 0.4 × S × σ√T` | Quick estimate; exact price is slightly higher |
| 32 | Delta | `Call Δ = e^(−qT) N(d1) · Put Δ = −e^(−qT) N(−d1)` | Premium change per ₹1 move; hedge ratio |
| 33 | Put delta from call delta | `Put Δ = Call Δ − e^(−qT)` | Same strike and expiry |
| 34 | Gamma | `Γ = e^(−qT) n(d1) ÷ (S σ √T)` | Rate of change of delta; peaks ATM near expiry |
| 35 | Vega | `Vega = S e^(−qT) n(d1) √T` | Premium change per 1 point of volatility |
| 36 | Theta | `Θ = −∂V/∂T` | Daily time decay; negative for long options |
| 37 | Rho | `Call ρ = K T e^(−rT) N(d2) · Put ρ = −K T e^(−rT) N(−d2)` | Higher rates help calls, hurt puts |
| 38 | One-day P&L (Profit and Loss) attribution | `P&L ≈ Δ × ΔS + ½ × Γ × ΔS² + Vega × Δσ + Θ × Days` | Explains why an option gained less than expected |
| 39 | Theta pays for gamma | `½ × σ² × S² × Γ ÷ 365 ≈ abs(Θ per day)` | Time decay compensates sellers for curvature risk |
| 40 | Option delta exposure | `Index units = Delta × Multiplier × Number of contracts` | Equivalent underlying exposure of an option position |
| 41 | Delta-hedger break-even move | `ΔS ≈ √(2 × abs(Θ) ÷ Γ)` | Bigger daily moves make short-option dealer lose |
| 42 | Implied volatility | `IV = σ such that Black-Scholes price = Market price` | IV (Implied Volatility); higher price means higher IV |
| 43 | Protective put max loss | `Max loss = (S − K + Premium) × Lot size` | Worst case, however far the market falls |
| 44 | Option hedge floor (exporter buys put) | `Floor = (Strike − Premium) × Foreign currency amount` | Guaranteed minimum revenue, upside retained |
| 45 | Forward hedge loss when exposure vanishes | `Loss = (Spot at maturity − Forward rate) × Amount sold forward` | Over-hedge turns a hedge into speculation |
| 46 | Covered call max profit | `Max profit = (K − Purchase price) + Premium` | Upside capped at strike |
| 47 | Covered call break-even | `BE = Purchase price − Premium` | Premium cushions small falls |
| 48 | Covered call monthly yield | `Yield = Premium ÷ Share price` | Income if shares are not called away |
| 49 | Covered put max profit | `Max profit = (Short price − K) + Premium` | Short stock + short put; rally loss unlimited |
| 50 | Covered put break-even | `BE = Short price + Premium` | Above this the position loses |

## Session 16: Securitisation, CDS (Credit Default Swap) and the 2008 Crisis

| # | Formula | Expression | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Basis point | `1 bp = 0.01% · 100 bp = 1%` | bp (Basis Point) is the unit for spreads |
| 2 | Pool interest income | `Pool interest = Pool size × Loan rate` | Cash available for the tranche waterfall |
| 3 | Tranche promised payment | `Due = Tranche size × Promised rate` | Senior and mezzanine have fixed claims |
| 4 | Cash waterfall | `Senior paid first → Mezzanine next → Equity = Pool cash − Senior paid − Mezz paid` | MBS (Mortgage-Backed Security) cash paid top-down; equity gets the residual, never negative |
| 5 | Equity tranche return | `Equity return = Residual cash ÷ Equity tranche size` | Highest return because it is paid last |
| 6 | Loss waterfall (subordination) | `Equity absorbs first losses → then Mezz → Senior loss = Pool loss − Equity − Mezz (if positive)` | Losses hit bottom-up; juniors protect seniors |
| 7 | Senior loss percentage | `Senior loss % = Senior loss ÷ Senior tranche size` | Damage to the "safe" layer |
| 8 | Expected loss with independent defaults | `Expected pool loss ≈ Default chance × Pool size` | Independent defaults cluster near average; not for common-mode failure |
| 9 | Credit spread | `Spread = Company bond yield − Government bond yield` | Market's default fear, in bp |

| 13 | CDS payout (cash settlement) | `Payout = Notional × (1 − Recovery rate) = Face value − Recovered amount` | Seller covers the loss on a credit event |
| 14 | Option budget in an MLN (Market-Linked Note) | `Option budget = Principal − Bond portion` | Higher rates leave more for upside |
| 15 | MLN maturity value with cap | `Value = Principal × (1 + min(Participation × max(0, Index return), Cap))` | Cap limits the best case. Without a cap, drop the min(…) |
| 16 | Simple annual return | `Simple = Total return ÷ Years` | 35% over 5 years = 7% a year simple (≈ 6.19% compound) |
