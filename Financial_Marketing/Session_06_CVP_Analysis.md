## 1. Two Ways to Prepare a Profit Statement

### 1A. Absorption (Full / Traditional) Costing
Both **fixed and variable production overheads** go into the product cost. Fixed production overhead is "absorbed" into each unit.

| Absorption costing statement | | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | XXX | |
| Less: Cost of goods sold | (XXX) | Cost of production + Opening stock − Closing stock |
| **Gross profit** | XXX | Sales − COGS |
| Add/(Less): Over/(Under)-absorbed OH | XXX | Actual fixed production OH vs amount absorbed |
| Less: Selling & admin overhead | (XXX) | Fixed + variable |
| **Net profit** | XXX | |

**Over/under-absorption:** If ₹1,80,000 of fixed overhead was absorbed into units but the actual overhead was ₹2,00,000, then ₹20,000 is **under-absorbed** and is **deducted**. If more was absorbed than actually spent, the excess (over-absorbed) is **added back**.

### 1B. Marginal (Variable) Costing
Costs are split by **behaviour** (variable vs fixed), not by function (production, admin, selling). **Fixed costs are period costs**: charged in full to the period, not spread over units.

| Marginal costing statement | | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | XXX | |
| Less: Variable cost | (XXX) | |
| **Contribution** | XXX | Sales − Variable cost |
| Less: Fixed cost | (XXX) | |
| **Profit** | XXX | Contribution − Fixed cost |

### 1C. Comparison

| | Absorption costing | Marginal costing |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed production OH | Part of product cost (sits in inventory) | Period cost (charged fully this period) |
| Classification of costs | By function | By behaviour |
| Key profit figure | Gross profit | Contribution |
| Main use | External reporting | Internal decisions (CVP) |

**Small example (extra, not on slides):** A firm produces 10,000 units and sells 8,000. Fixed production OH = ₹2,00,000, i.e. ₹20/unit under absorption. There is no opening stock.
- Absorption costing: closing stock of 2,000 units carries 2,000 × ₹20 = ₹40,000 of fixed OH into next year.
- Marginal costing: the full ₹2,00,000 is charged this year.
- So **absorption profit is ₹40,000 higher** this year. When production = sales, both methods give the same profit.

> 💡 **In simple words:** Absorption mein fixed cost ka kuch hissa closing stock ke saath "agle saal chala jaata hai", isliye jab stock badhta hai to absorption wala profit zyada dikhta hai. Marginal mein poora fixed cost isi saal kat jaata hai.

## 2. What is CVP Analysis?

**Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) analysis** is a management accounting technique that shows how changes in **costs, sales volume and selling price** affect **profit**. It rests on separating costs into **fixed and variable**, and then using those relationships to find the break-even point and beyond.

It answers questions like:
- How many units must we sell to **break even**?
- How many units must we sell to earn a **target profit**?
- What happens to profit if **volume** rises or falls?
- What happens if the **selling price** changes?
- What happens if **variable or fixed costs** change?
- How far can sales **fall before we make a loss**?

> 📌 **Usual assumptions (extra, not on slides):** selling price and variable cost per unit stay constant, fixed costs stay fixed within the relevant range, and production = sales. That's why every CVP line is a straight line.

## 3. Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Selling Price (SP)** | Revenue per unit |
| **Variable Cost (VC)** | Cost per unit that changes with volume |
| **Contribution Margin (CM)** | SP − VC per unit: what each unit contributes towards fixed costs, then profit |
| **Fixed Costs (FC)** | Costs that don't change with volume (rent, salaries, etc.) |
| **Break-Even Point (BEP)** | Volume at which **Total Revenue = Total Cost**, so **Profit = 0** (no profit, no loss) |

**Break-even analysis** finds the sales level at which total revenue equals total costs.

> 💡 **In simple words:** Har unit bechne pe jo contribution bachta hai, wo pehle fixed cost ka "gaddha" bharta hai. Gaddha bhar gaya to BEP aa gaya, uske baad har unit ka contribution seedha profit hai.

> ⚠️ The "Key Terms" slide starts with a broken, repeated sentence ("Key Terms: is a management accounting technique…") copied from the previous slide, and shows "Total Revenue = Total Cost (SP)" with a stray "(SP)". The correct condition is simply **Total Revenue = Total Cost**.

## 4. Key Formulae ⭐

| # | Measure | Formula | Slide example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sales Revenue** | SP per unit × Units sold | ₹2,000 × 5,000 = **₹1,00,00,000** |
| 2 | **Total Variable Cost (TVC)** | VC per unit (V) × Units (Q) | VC/unit = 1,200 + 500 + 300 = ₹2,000; × 10,000 = **₹2,00,00,000** |
| 3 | **Contribution** | Sales − Variable cost = (SP − VC) × Units = CM per unit × Units | (500 − 300) = ₹200/unit; × 10,000 = **₹20,00,000** |
| 4 | **Profit** | Total contribution − Total fixed cost | |
| 5 | **P/V Ratio** | (Contribution ÷ Sales) × 100 | 200 ÷ 500 × 100 = **40%** |
| 6 | **BEP (units)** | Fixed cost ÷ Contribution per unit | |
| 7 | **BEP (sales value)** | Fixed cost ÷ P/V ratio | 2,00,000 ÷ 40% = **₹5,00,000** |
| 8 | **MoS (units)** | Actual units sold − Break-even units | 10,000 − 6,000 = **4,000 units** |
| 9 | **MoS (value)** | Actual sales − Break-even sales | 10,00,000 − 6,00,000 = **₹4,00,000** |
| 10 | **MoS %** | (MoS ÷ Actual sales) × 100 | 4,00,000 ÷ 10,00,000 = **40%** |
| 11 | **Units for target profit** | (Fixed cost + Target profit) ÷ CM per unit | (12,00,000 + 8,00,000) ÷ 200 = **10,000 units** |

**Reading the key ratios:**
- **P/V ratio 40%** → every ₹100 of sales generates ₹40 of contribution. A higher P/V ratio means each extra sale adds more profit.
- **Margin of Safety** → how much sales can fall before the business slips below BEP. **Higher MoS = lower risk.** A 40% MoS means sales can drop by 40% before losses start.

> 💡 **In simple words:** MoS ek "cushion" hai. Kitni sales gir sakti hai bina loss mein gaye, wahi margin of safety hai.

> ⚠️ The slides number both BEP formulas as "6." (units and sales value), so the later numbering is off by one. This table numbers them in order.

## 5. The Break-Even Chart (slide illustration)

**Data:** SP = $50/unit, VC = $30/unit, FC = $40,000.
- CM = 50 − 30 = **$20/unit**
- BEP (units) = 40,000 ÷ 20 = **2,000 units**
- BEP (sales) = 2,000 × 50 = **$1,00,000**

**How to read the chart:**

| Line / area | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Revenue line (blue) | Starts at $0 and rises steeply: +$50 per unit |
| Total cost line (orange) | Starts at **$40,000 (the fixed cost)** and rises more gently: +$30 per unit |
| Fixed cost line (dashed grey) | Flat at $40,000 at every volume |
| Crossing point | **Break-even point**: 2,000 units, $1,00,000 sales |
| Red triangle (left of BEP) | Loss zone: its height = the loss at that volume |
| Green triangle (right of BEP) | Profit zone: the gap widens by $20 (the CM) per extra unit |

**Extended to 3,000 units sold:**

| Item | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 3,000 × 50 | $1,50,000 |
| Total cost | 40,000 + 3,000 × 30 | $1,30,000 |
| **Profit** | 1,50,000 − 1,30,000 | **$20,000** |
| MoS (units) | 3,000 − 2,000 | 1,000 units |
| MoS (value) | 1,000 × 50 | $50,000 |
| MoS ratio | 50,000 ÷ 1,50,000 | **33.3%** |

✔️ Check: Profit = MoS units × CM = 1,000 × 20 = $20,000 ✅

> ⚠️ This illustration uses **$** while the rest of the deck uses ₹. The logic is identical.

## 6. Handy Shortcuts (extra, not on slides)

These follow directly from the formulas above and save time in exams:

| Shortcut | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Profit = MoS (value) × P/V ratio** | Sales above BEP don't need to cover fixed costs, so their whole contribution is profit |
| **Profit = Sales × P/V ratio − FC** | Contribution = Sales × P/V ratio |
| **P/V ratio = Change in profit ÷ Change in sales** | FC is unchanged between two periods, so any extra profit comes from extra contribution |
| **SP = CM per unit ÷ P/V ratio** | P/V ratio = CM ÷ SP |

✔️ Check with slide numbers: MoS value 10,000 − 6,000 = 4,000 units × ₹500 = ₹20,00,000; × 40% = ₹8,00,000 = Profit (20L contribution − 12L FC) ✅

## 7. Solved Example: What-If Analysis

**Base case:** SP = ₹500, VC = ₹300, FC = ₹12,00,000, Sales = 10,000 units.
- CM = ₹200/unit, Contribution = ₹20,00,000, **Profit = ₹8,00,000**
- BEP = 12,00,000 ÷ 200 = **6,000 units**, MoS = 4,000 units (40%)

| Change (one at a time) | New CM | New profit | New BEP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price +10% (₹550) | 250 | 25,00,000 − 12,00,000 = **₹13,00,000** | 12,00,000 ÷ 250 = **4,800 units** |
| VC +₹50 (₹350) | 150 | 15,00,000 − 12,00,000 = **₹3,00,000** | 12,00,000 ÷ 150 = **8,000 units** |
| FC +₹4,00,000 (₹16,00,000) | 200 | 20,00,000 − 16,00,000 = **₹4,00,000** | 16,00,000 ÷ 200 = **8,000 units** |
| Volume −20% (8,000 units) | 200 | 16,00,000 − 12,00,000 = **₹4,00,000** | **6,000 units (unchanged)** |

**Lessons:**
- **Price and VC** change the CM per unit, so they move **both profit and BEP**.
- **Fixed cost** changes the BEP but **not the P/V ratio**.
- **Volume** changes profit but **not the BEP**: BEP depends only on FC and CM.
- A 10% price rise lifted profit by 62.5% (8L → 13L). Small price moves have a big effect on profit when margins are thin.

---

## 8. Quick Revision 🧠

- **Absorption costing:** fixed production OH sits in product cost → gross profit. **Marginal costing:** fixed costs are period costs → contribution.
- **Contribution = Sales − VC** · **Profit = Contribution − FC**
- **P/V ratio = Contribution ÷ Sales × 100**
- **BEP units = FC ÷ CM per unit** · **BEP sales = FC ÷ P/V ratio**
- **MoS = Actual − BEP** · **MoS % = MoS ÷ Actual sales × 100** · Higher MoS = lower risk
- **Target units = (FC + Target profit) ÷ CM per unit**
- **Profit = MoS × P/V ratio** (shortcut)
- On the chart, the total cost line starts at the FC level; the BEP is where revenue crosses total cost.

## 9. MCQs (Mid–Advanced Level) 📝

**Q1.** A product sells for ₹800 with a variable cost of ₹560 per unit. Fixed costs are ₹6,00,000. What is the break-even point in units?
- A) 3,500 units
- B) 2,500 units
- C) 750 units
- D) 1,071 units

> ✅ **Answer: B.** CM = 800 − 560 = ₹240. BEP = 6,00,000 ÷ 240 = **2,500 units**. Option D divides by the variable cost and Option C divides by the selling price instead of the contribution. 📖 Section 4

**Q2.** For the same product (SP ₹800, VC ₹560), what is the P/V ratio?
- A) 70%
- B) 30%
- C) 24%
- D) 42.9%

> ✅ **Answer: B.** P/V = 240 ÷ 800 × 100 = **30%**. Option A is the variable cost ratio, and Option D divides contribution by variable cost instead of sales. 📖 Section 4

**Q3.** Fixed costs are ₹9,00,000 and the P/V ratio is 36%. What are break-even sales?
- A) ₹14,06,250
- B) ₹12,24,000
- C) ₹3,24,000
- D) ₹25,00,000

> ✅ **Answer: D.** BEP sales = 9,00,000 ÷ 0.36 = **₹25,00,000**. Option C multiplies instead of dividing, and Option A divides by the variable cost ratio (64%). 📖 Section 4

**Q4.** Actual sales are ₹40,00,000 and break-even sales are ₹30,00,000. What is the margin of safety percentage?
- A) 75%
- B) 10%
- C) 33.3%
- D) 25%

> ✅ **Answer: D.** MoS = 40L − 30L = ₹10L. MoS % = 10 ÷ 40 × 100 = **25%**. Option C wrongly divides by break-even sales; the base is always actual sales. 📖 Section 4

**Q5.** SP is ₹1,200, VC is ₹750, fixed costs are ₹18,00,000, and management wants a profit of ₹9,00,000. How many units must be sold?
- A) 6,000 units
- B) 3,600 units
- C) 2,000 units
- D) 4,000 units

> ✅ **Answer: A.** CM = 1,200 − 750 = ₹450. Units = (18,00,000 + 9,00,000) ÷ 450 = 27,00,000 ÷ 450 = **6,000 units**. Option D is only the BEP, and Option B divides by VC instead of CM. 📖 Section 4

**Q6.** Sales are ₹50,00,000, the P/V ratio is 25% and fixed costs are ₹8,00,000. What is the profit?
- A) ₹4,50,000
- B) ₹2,00,000
- C) ₹12,50,000
- D) ₹42,00,000

> ✅ **Answer: A.** Contribution = 50L × 25% = ₹12.5L. Profit = 12.5L − 8L = **₹4.5L**. Option C is the contribution, not profit. 📖 Section 6

**Q7.** A company sells 10,000 units at ₹500 with VC ₹300 and FC ₹12,00,000. If it cuts the price by 10% and volume does not change, what is the new profit?
- A) ₹3,00,000
- B) ₹13,00,000
- C) ₹7,20,000
- D) ₹8,00,000

> ✅ **Answer: A.** New SP = ₹450, CM = ₹150. Contribution = 15,00,000. Profit = 15L − 12L = **₹3,00,000**. Option C wrongly reduces profit by just 10%; the whole price cut comes out of the contribution. 📖 Section 7

**Q8.** In the same case (price cut to ₹450, CM ₹150), how many units must be sold to keep the original profit of ₹8,00,000?
- A) 13,334 units
- B) 8,000 units
- C) 11,000 units
- D) 10,000 units

> ✅ **Answer: A.** Units = (12,00,000 + 8,00,000) ÷ 150 = 13,333.3, so **13,334 units** (round up). Option C assumes volume needs to rise only 10%, and Option B is the new BEP. A 10% price cut needs about 33% more volume. 📖 Section 7

**Q9.** Which change will **NOT** change the break-even point in units?
- A) A rise in fixed costs
- B) A fall in sales volume
- C) A rise in variable cost per unit
- D) A fall in selling price

> ✅ **Answer: B.** BEP = FC ÷ CM per unit. Sales volume is in neither, so it changes profit and MoS but not BEP. 📖 Section 7

**Q10.** Fixed costs rise by 20% while SP and VC per unit stay the same. What happens?
- A) P/V ratio falls and BEP rises
- B) P/V ratio is unchanged and BEP rises by 20%
- C) P/V ratio and BEP are both unchanged
- D) P/V ratio rises and BEP falls

> ✅ **Answer: B.** P/V ratio depends only on SP and VC, so it doesn't move. BEP = FC ÷ CM, so a 20% rise in FC raises BEP by 20%. 📖 Section 7

**Q11.** A company's margin of safety is ₹20,00,000 and its P/V ratio is 40%. What is its profit?
- A) ₹50,00,000
- B) ₹8,00,000
- C) ₹20,00,000
- D) ₹12,00,000

> ✅ **Answer: B.** Profit = MoS × P/V = 20L × 40% = **₹8L**. Sales above BEP don't need to cover fixed costs, so all their contribution is profit. Option A divides instead of multiplying. 📖 Section 6

**Q12.** A firm's profit is ₹6,00,000, its P/V ratio is 30% and its MoS is 25% of sales. What are its actual sales?
- A) ₹24,00,000
- B) ₹80,00,000
- C) ₹60,00,000
- D) ₹20,00,000

> ✅ **Answer: B.** MoS value = Profit ÷ P/V = 6L ÷ 0.30 = ₹20L. Actual sales = 20L ÷ 0.25 = **₹80L**. Check: BEP sales = 60L, FC = 60L × 30% = 18L, Contribution = 80L × 30% = 24L, Profit = 24 − 18 = 6L ✅. Option D is the MoS and Option C is BEP sales. 📖 Section 6

**Q13.** In Year 1, sales were ₹20,00,000 and profit ₹2,00,000. In Year 2, sales were ₹30,00,000 and profit ₹5,00,000. Fixed costs did not change. What are the fixed costs?
- A) ₹2,00,000
- B) ₹6,00,000
- C) ₹4,00,000
- D) ₹9,00,000

> ✅ **Answer: C.** P/V = Δprofit ÷ Δsales = 3L ÷ 10L = 30%. Year 1 contribution = 20L × 30% = 6L, so FC = 6L − 2L = **₹4L**. Check Year 2: 30L × 30% − 4L = 5L ✅. Option B is Year 1 contribution, and Option D is Year 2 contribution. 📖 Section 6

**Q14.** A company produces 10,000 units and sells 8,000 (no opening stock). Fixed production overhead of ₹3,00,000 is absorbed at ₹30 per unit. How do the profits under the two methods compare?
- A) Absorption costing profit is ₹3,00,000 higher
- B) Marginal costing profit is ₹60,000 higher
- C) Both profits are the same
- D) Absorption costing profit is ₹60,000 higher

> ✅ **Answer: D.** Under absorption, the 2,000 units in closing stock carry 2,000 × ₹30 = **₹60,000** of fixed overhead into next year. Marginal costing charges the full ₹3L this year, so its profit is lower. 📖 Section 1C

**Q15.** Under marginal costing, how is fixed production overhead treated?
- A) It is ignored in the profit statement
- B) It is split between cost of sales and closing stock
- C) It is charged in full as a period cost
- D) It is absorbed into the cost of each unit produced

> ✅ **Answer: C.** Marginal costing treats fixed costs as **period costs** charged in full to the period. Option D and Option B describe absorption costing. 📖 Section 1B

**Q16.** Under absorption costing, fixed overhead absorbed into production is ₹4,50,000 but actual fixed overhead is ₹5,00,000. How is the difference treated in the profit statement?
- A) Over-absorbed ₹50,000, deducted from gross profit
- B) Under-absorbed ₹50,000, added to gross profit
- C) Over-absorbed ₹50,000, added to gross profit
- D) Under-absorbed ₹50,000, deducted from gross profit

> ✅ **Answer: D.** Less was absorbed than actually spent, so ₹50,000 is **under-absorbed**. That extra cost has not been charged yet, so it is deducted. 📖 Section 1A

**Q17.** On a break-even chart, where does the total cost line start on the vertical axis?
- A) At zero, like the revenue line
- B) At the variable cost per unit
- C) At the fixed cost level
- D) At the break-even point

> ✅ **Answer: C.** At zero units there is still the full fixed cost, so total cost starts at the FC level (e.g. $40,000) and rises by VC per unit. 📖 Section 5

**Q18.** Using the slide illustration (SP $50, VC $30, FC $40,000), what are the profit and MoS % if the company sells 2,500 units?
- A) $10,000 profit; MoS 20%
- B) $10,000 profit; MoS 25%
- C) $20,000 profit; MoS 33.3%
- D) $50,000 profit; MoS 20%

> ✅ **Answer: A.** Profit = 2,500 × 20 − 40,000 = **$10,000**. MoS = 2,500 − 2,000 = 500 units, so MoS % = 500 ÷ 2,500 = **20%**. Option B divides by BEP units instead of actual units, and Option C is the 3,000-unit case. 📖 Section 5

**Q19.** A product earns a contribution of ₹40 per unit and its P/V ratio is 25%. What is its selling price?
- A) ₹50
- B) ₹100
- C) ₹120
- D) ₹160

> ✅ **Answer: D.** SP = CM ÷ P/V = 40 ÷ 0.25 = **₹160** (so VC = ₹120). Option C is the variable cost, not the price. 📖 Section 6

**Q20.** Company X has a margin of safety of 10% and Company Y has 45%. Which statement is correct?
- A) Company X carries more risk of making a loss
- B) Company Y carries more risk of making a loss
- C) Both carry equal risk because MoS does not measure risk
- D) Company X must have higher profit

> ✅ **Answer: A.** A 10% fall in sales pushes X to break-even, while Y can lose 45% of sales first. **Lower MoS = higher risk.** 📖 Section 4

**Q21.** SP is ₹1,000, VC ₹600 and FC ₹20,00,000. If variable cost rises by 20%, what is the new break-even point?
- A) 5,000 units
- B) 6,000 units
- C) 7,143 units
- D) 4,167 units

> ✅ **Answer: C.** New VC = ₹720, CM = ₹280. BEP = 20,00,000 ÷ 280 = 7,142.9, so **7,143 units** (up from 5,000). Option A is the old BEP, and Option D is the BEP if VC fell by 20%. 📖 Section 7

**Q22.** Which statement about contribution is correct?
- A) Contribution is calculated after deducting fixed costs
- B) Contribution per unit falls as volume rises
- C) Contribution first covers fixed costs; only the excess becomes profit
- D) Contribution is the same as gross profit under absorption costing

> ✅ **Answer: C.** Contribution = Sales − VC. It first covers fixed costs, and only after BEP does it become profit. Option D confuses it with gross profit (which deducts fixed production OH), and CM per unit is constant under CVP assumptions. 📖 Section 3
